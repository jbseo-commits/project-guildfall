# WORLD BIBLE — THE LIVING CARAVAN

> **Status:** CANONICAL DRAFT V1
>
> This document turns the locked Living Caravan fantasy into a usable world.
> The emotional foundation is locked by `docs/DECISIONS.md`.
> Specific proper nouns and large-scale lore introduced here may still be revised if playtests show that they weaken the game.
>
> This file must not silently lock D002, D003, D004, or the permanent loss/recovery economy.

---

# 1. One-sentence world premise

> **The world is slowly becoming Still. The last living sanctuaries survive by moving, carrying beautiful beings whose bodies and relationships literally keep the sanctuary alive.**

PROJECT GUILDFALL is not a story about assembling a party and clearing dungeons.

It is a story about **keeping a moving life-form alive while the world around it forgets how to live**.

---

# 2. The catastrophe — The Stillfall

Long ago, the world was threaded by a living network called **the Pulse**.

The Pulse was not a god, machine, or simple ley-line system. It was the world's shared capacity to:
- heal;
- adapt;
- remember;
- migrate;
- mutate;
- form relationships between living things.

Cities were once built around Pulse currents. Forests moved over generations. Rivers changed course in response to famine. Some buildings were cultivated rather than constructed.

Then the Pulse fractured.

The event is remembered as **the Stillfall**.

After the Stillfall:
- some places became unable to change;
- ecosystems froze into obsessive repeating behaviors;
- roads began leading to the same places regardless of direction;
- dead settlements retained routines without people;
- animals developed ritualized predation cycles;
- weather became trapped in local patterns;
- memory and biology began leaking into one another.

The world did not end.

It became **wrongly stable**.

That is the central horror of the setting.

---

# 3. Stillness

**Stillness** is not ordinary death.

It is the condition in which something loses the ability to meaningfully change.

A Still forest may keep growing the same branch shapes forever.

A Still town may ring its bells every evening despite having no inhabitants.

A Still creature may repeat the same hunt for centuries.

A Still person may retain speech and memory while being unable to form a new desire.

Stillness is frightening because it can look beautiful.

Perfect symmetry, flawless repetition, preserved faces, preserved cities, preserved grief.

The Living Caravan exists in opposition to this.

It is messy, relational, mobile, costly, and alive.

---

# 4. The Living Caravan

The player's caravan is one of the rare surviving **Wandering Sanctuaries**.

A Wandering Sanctuary is not a wagon train.

It is a living mobile habitat grown around a central organism called the **Hearthheart**.

The Hearthheart:
- changes shape slowly;
- grows rooms, membranes, roots, lantern-organs, storage cavities, and pathways;
- reacts to its inhabitants;
- remembers injuries;
- cannot remain in one place for too long without attracting Stillness;
- is incomplete without the beings who live within it.

The sanctuary is therefore both:
- **home**
and
- **body**.

The caravan's members are not passengers.

They are part of its physiology.

---

# 5. Why the caravan must keep moving

A sanctuary that stops moving begins to accumulate **Still pressure**.

At first:
- flowers stop closing at night;
- shadows repeat;
- conversations feel familiar;
- healing slows.

Then:
- rooms stop changing;
- memories become fixed;
- inhabitants begin repeating habits;
- the Hearthheart forms crystal-like scars.

Eventually, the sanctuary becomes another beautiful dead place.

This gives the journey a permanent existential reason:

> **Movement is life.**

The caravan travels not because a quest marker says so, but because settling forever is a form of death.

---

# 6. The player's role

The player is known provisionally as the **Keeper**.

The Keeper is not a battlefield commander issuing every swing.

The Keeper:
- chooses where the sanctuary travels;
- decides who faces danger;
- shapes relationships;
- assigns shelter and proximity;
- makes difficult recovery decisions;
- decides which risks the caravan accepts;
- decides what kind of organism the sanctuary becomes.

The Keeper's power is **authorship before commitment**.

Once danger begins, the caravan's inhabitants react according to:
- instinct;
- trust;
- fear;
- resonance;
- injury;
- habit;
- relationships;
- the structure the Keeper has created.

This fiction supports automatic combat.

---

# 7. The Inhabitants

The caravan's inhabitants are called **Resonants** in current working terminology.

A Resonant is a being whose body still carries a strong fragment of the old Pulse.

They are rare because most post-Stillfall life has become less adaptive, not more.

Resonants tend to be:
- beautiful in an uncanny way;
- physically distinctive;
- dangerous;
- difficult to categorize as fully human, monster, spirit, or construct;
- strongly affected by relationships.

A Resonant does not simply have a combat class.

Each one performs a **living function** for the sanctuary.

Examples:
- defense;
- memory;
- navigation;
- healing;
- dreaming;
- concealment;
- hunting;
- pollination;
- temperature control;
- emotional regulation;
- scent;
- mutation;
- warning;
- digestion of corrupted materials.

This is why losing a member hurts mechanically and emotionally.

The sanctuary literally becomes less complete.

---

# 8. The core cast

## Vael — The Ward

Vael is the sanctuary's defensive reflex.

When someone is threatened, Vael's body reacts before thought fully catches up.

Narrative function:
- protection;
- bodily trust;
- the cost of always being the one who takes the hit.

World function:
- Vael stabilizes the caravan's outer membranes during travel;
- the sanctuary feels safer when Vael is awake;
- doors and passageways physically tense when Vael senses danger.

Emotional contradiction:
> Vael is most useful when hurt.

That should become painful over time.

---

## Seris — The Moon Memory

Seris carries a rare form of Pulse memory that appears under moonlight.

Seris can perceive:
- old roads;
- erased settlements;
- places that used to exist;
- fragments of possible routes.

Narrative function:
- memory;
- navigation;
- longing;
- knowledge that may be better forgotten.

World function:
- the caravan can find hidden passages and safe migrations because of Seris;
- some factions want Seris alive specifically because this ability can reveal lost routes and sealed sanctuaries.

Emotional contradiction:
> The more Seris remembers for everyone else, the less certain Seris becomes about which memories are truly personal.

---

## Mirel — The Bloom

Mirel converts damaged Pulse residue into living tissue.

Mirel does not merely "cast healing magic."

Healing is closer to accelerated ecological repair:
- flowers;
- fibers;
- spores;
- warm tissue;
- temporary symbiotic growth.

Narrative function:
- restoration;
- care;
- overextension;
- the fear of being valued only for saving others.

World function:
- Mirel helps the Hearthheart regrow damaged chambers;
- Mirel can stabilize injured Resonants;
- Mirel's presence allows the caravan to survive environments that would otherwise poison it.

Emotional contradiction:
> Mirel can save almost anything except without paying with something living.

The permanent cost model is not yet locked.

---

# 9. The Hearthheart

The Hearthheart is the sanctuary's central living core.

It should not behave like a chatty mascot.

It communicates through:
- room growth;
- temperature;
- vibrations;
- light;
- dreams;
- rearranged pathways;
- the way it places objects near people.

The player should gradually learn its personality through behavior.

Possible interpretation:
- it may be an animal;
- a fragment of the old Pulse;
- a designed organism;
- a sanctuary that learned to want.

The truth should remain partially mysterious.

---

# 10. The world's major human response to the Stillfall

Different societies survived by choosing different relationships with change.

## A. The White Index

A civilization of surveyors, surgeons, archivists, and collectors.

Their belief:
> Change is dangerous because uncontrolled adaptation caused the world's collapse.

They catalog anomalies and stabilize them.

In practice, they:
- capture Resonants;
- remove useful organs or traits;
- preserve rare bodies;
- dissect living sanctuaries;
- convert living functions into predictable tools.

They are not cartoon villains.

Their cities are safe.

Their medicine works.

Their roads are reliable.

People willingly live under them because the alternative is terrifying.

Their horror is that they treat living uniqueness as a problem to be solved.

The White Index is the natural institutional antagonist to the caravan.

---

## B. The Hearthless

Nomadic communities that refuse permanent settlement.

Unlike the player's caravan, most Hearthless do not have a living sanctuary.

They survive by:
- moving constantly;
- dismantling camps every few days;
- trading route memories;
- using portable rituals to avoid Still pressure.

Some view Wandering Sanctuaries as holy.

Others view them as dangerous predators that consume too many resources.

The Hearthless provide:
- allies;
- merchants;
- witnesses;
- rivals;
- stories about other failed sanctuaries.

---

## C. The Root Cities

Old cities that survived by anchoring themselves to controlled Pulse fragments.

They are stable, beautiful, and deeply conservative.

Each Root City has a local law of survival.

Examples:
- one city forbids anyone from sleeping twice in the same room;
- one city rearranges family names every season;
- one city burns a district every decade to prevent permanent Still patterns;
- one city sacrifices all mirrors.

Root Cities are not one faction.

They are a source of:
- regional identity;
- difficult hospitality;
- local quests;
- political pressure;
- cultural contrast with the caravan.

---

## D. The Choir of Stillness

A spiritual movement that believes the Stillfall was not a disaster.

Their belief:
> Pain exists because living things continue to change.

They seek perfect preservation.

Some Choir communities peacefully enter ritual stasis.

Others actively spread Stillness.

Their strongest members become eerily beautiful and almost impossible to surprise because they have removed uncertainty from themselves.

The Choir represents a philosophical antagonist:

> Is painful change actually better than perfect preservation?

---

# 11. Non-human threats

The world should not be filled with generic fantasy monsters.

Enemies should look like **failed relationships with change**.

## Repeaters
Creatures trapped in one successful behavior.

A predator may repeat the exact same attack pattern because it once worked.

Gameplay implication:
- readable patterns;
- adaptation opportunities.

## Grafts
Multiple organisms fused by unstable Pulse pressure.

They may share:
- limbs;
- senses;
- pain;
- targeting behavior.

Gameplay implication:
- linked enemies;
- damage transfer;
- shared status.

## Hollow Saints
Preserved people whose personalities were gradually replaced by a single ideal.

They can be:
- beautiful;
- articulate;
- terrifying.

Gameplay implication:
- predictable but overwhelming behavior;
- resistance to disruption;
- strong narrative encounters.

## Strays
Former sanctuary organisms that survived after their home died.

They are emotionally important because they show what the caravan's own inhabitants could become after catastrophic loss.

# 12. Regions

The first release should not require dozens of biomes.

Each region should have:
- a survival rule;
- a visual identity;
- a relationship to Stillness;
- a distinctive enemy behavior;
- a reason for the caravan to enter.

## Region 1 — Lanternwood

A forest where paths rearrange after sunset.

Visual identity:
- warm lanterns;
- deep blue foliage;
- violet/cyan growth;
- old roadside shelters;
- damp soil;
- half-swallowed ruins.

Stillness form:
- repeated paths;
- repeated nights;
- hunters following old routes forever.

Purpose:
- tutorial region;
- teaches threat reading, protection, healing, and route memory.

---

## Region 2 — Glassroot Basin

A valley filled with transparent mineralized roots.

Visual identity:
- pale stone;
- glass veins;
- reflected silhouettes;
- trapped old architecture.

Stillness form:
- memories become physical echoes.

Purpose:
- introduces false information, duplicated threats, memory-based encounters.

---

## Region 3 — The Red Orchard

A cultivated region abandoned after every tree began producing the same perfect fruit.

Visual identity:
- crimson leaves;
- ceremonial irrigation;
- beautiful rot;
- long rows of identical trees.

Stillness form:
- abundance without variation.

Purpose:
- introduces temptation, resource abundance, and costs hidden behind stability.

---

## Region 4 — Salt Sky

A high plateau where wind carries crystallized fragments of old voices.

Visual identity:
- pale grass;
- blue-white salt;
- enormous sky;
- suspended debris.

Stillness form:
- language and memory repeating in weather.

Purpose:
- late-run emotional region;
- character memories / relationships become mechanically relevant.

These names and order are provisional until the run structure is locked.

---

# 13. Resources should come from fiction

Avoid generic "gold / mana / shards" wherever possible.

Candidate resource families:

## Warmth
Immediate vitality / shelter capacity.

## Sap
Living construction / repair material.

## Echo
Memory residue used for route reading, relationship growth, or restoration.

## Husk
Stable dead material useful for armor, trade, or dangerous preservation.

## Favor
Not reputation points; specific relational trust with settlements or groups.

Exact economy is not locked.

The principle is:

> Resources should feel like things a living caravan would actually need.

---

# 14. Why people join

A Resonant should rarely join because the player bought a recruitment token.

Possible reasons:
- rescued from the White Index;
- sanctuary recognizes them;
- they are dying without a compatible ecosystem;
- they choose the caravan after a moral decision;
- they follow another character;
- they are an exile;
- they are a former enemy;
- they are the last survivor of another sanctuary.

Recruitment should create story.

---

# 15. Why people leave or become lost

Loss should not only mean HP reached zero.

Future narrative states may include:
- death;
- separation;
- capture;
- voluntary departure;
- transformation;
- Still infection;
- memory collapse;
- sanctuary rejection;
- sacrifice.

The permanent gameplay rules are not locked.

The world supports multiple emotionally distinct kinds of loss.

---

# 16. Restoration in the fiction

Restoration is possible because the Pulse can preserve relational traces.

A person may leave behind:
- biological residue;
- a memory echo;
- a resonance pattern;
- a room that still reacts to them;
- relationships that remember their shape.

This creates a fiction for expensive recovery without turning death into a cheap reload.

Possible restoration costs:
- another character's memory;
- a large section of the Hearthheart;
- a rare route detour;
- lost time;
- a permanent scar;
- a sacrificed ecosystem function.

The exact mechanic must be decided later with the user.

---

# 17. The central long-term mystery

The caravan is incomplete.

Several chambers inside the Hearthheart exist but do not open.

Seris occasionally remembers roads that no longer exist.

Vael reacts defensively to symbols no one recognizes.

Mirel's flowers sometimes bloom toward empty space.

The recurring mystery:

> **Was this sanctuary built to survive the Stillfall — or did it exist before the Stillfall and somehow cause part of it?**

The game should not answer this immediately.

It gives the journey a reason to move beyond pure survival.

---

# 18. First-act narrative engine

The early story should be intimate before becoming cosmic.

The first act is about:

> **three people learning whether they are a party, a family, or parts of the same organism.**

Early narrative should focus on:
- protection;
- trust;
- dependence;
- fear of being useful only for one function;
- the first time the player chooses a person over efficiency.

The larger mystery enters gradually.

---

# 19. Tutorial story premise

Working title:

> **Day 1 — The Leaving Night**

The caravan has been dormant in Lanternwood.

Something has found it.

A White Index tracking unit is searching for **Seris**, whose Moon Memory can reveal lost sanctuary routes.

The player must:
1. understand the enemy's intent;
2. arrange who protects whom;
3. COMMIT;
4. watch the ecosystem respond;
5. understand why the outcome happened;
6. choose where to flee next.

This converts the existing tutorial mechanics into story.

The first fight should not feel like "tutorial enemies spawned."

It should feel like:
> **someone came for one of ours.**

---

# 20. Tone

The setting should feel:

- intimate before epic;
- warm inside, dangerous outside;
- beautiful without becoming clean;
- melancholic without becoming hopeless;
- strange without becoming incomprehensible;
- character-driven rather than lore-dump-driven.

The caravan itself should feel like the safest place in the game.

That safety is why threats matter.

---

# 21. Visual language

## Caravan interior
- warm amber light;
- cloth;
- wood;
- grown bone / root structures;
- flowers;
- hanging objects;
- personal clutter;
- soft asymmetry.

## Outside world
- deep blue / violet;
- cold mineral light;
- repeating geometry;
- eerie preservation;
- beautiful environmental danger.

## Stillness
Avoid generic black corruption.

Use:
- symmetry;
- repetition;
- frozen motion;
- immaculate surfaces;
- preserved expressions;
- patterns that repeat too perfectly.

## Living Pulse
Avoid generic green healing glow.

Use:
- breathing light;
- organic filaments;
- warmth;
- asymmetrical growth;
- small changes over time;
- relational lines / resonance.

---

# 22. Writing rules

Dialogue should not explain the lore unless a character has a reason to.

Prefer:
- personal observation;
- physical details;
- conflict;
- implication.

Bad:
> "The Stillfall shattered the Pulse 200 years ago."

Better:
> "That bell rang yesterday. Same note. Same pause. Same bird leaving the tower. We don't camp here twice."

Characters should talk like people who live in the world.

---

# 23. Lore delivery

Use:
- environmental details;
- changing rooms in the caravan;
- route events;
- injuries;
- camp conversations;
- character reactions;
- enemy behavior;
- recovered objects.

Avoid:
- giant codex dumps;
- mandatory encyclopedia reading;
- prologue narration longer than the player's first meaningful choice.

---

# 24. Worldbuilding constraints for gameplay

Worldbuilding must create gameplay opportunities.

A lore concept is strongest when it can become:
- an encounter;
- a targeting behavior;
- a route choice;
- a relationship consequence;
- a loss/recovery choice;
- a visual tell.

Examples:

**Stillness** → repeating enemy pattern.

**Resonance** → conditional ally reaction.

**Moon Memory** → route prediction.

**Hearthheart injury** → fewer recovery options.

**Root City law** → regional rule modifier.

# 26. North-star emotional test

A worldbuilding element belongs in PROJECT GUILDFALL if it strengthens at least one of these feelings:

> **“This place is alive.”**

> **“These people need each other.”**

> **“I chose this relationship.”**

> **“I don't want to lose them.”**

> **“Keeping them may cost me the run.”**

> **“When I commit, the organism I built comes alive.”**

If lore does not strengthen the game, cut it.
