> **Status: WORKING CANON / PROVISIONAL**
>
> This document turns the locked Living Caravan fantasy into a usable world.
> It does **not** override open major decisions in `docs/DECISIONS.md`.

---

# 1. One-sentence world premise

> **The world has been cut into dying regions, and the player leads a wandering living sanctuary whose inhabitants must keep moving because every place that can save them also wants something from them.**

The world should feel:
- beautiful enough to want to stay in;
- dangerous enough that staying is impossible;
- intimate enough that losing one companion changes the whole ecosystem;
- uncanny enough that the Living Caravan never feels like an ordinary adventuring party.

---

# 2. The old world and the Severance

Long ago, the world was linked by a living network called the **World Vein**.

The World Vein was not a road or a ley-line in the ordinary sense.

It was a circulatory system:
- forests shared memory through roots;
- cities harvested warmth from mineral organs below them;
- moonlight could be stored in living crystal;
- certain beings could sense danger, pain, or desire across great distances;
- ecosystems behaved less like isolated species and more like organs in one body.

Then came an event remembered as **the Severance**.

The World Vein ruptured.

The exact cause is deliberately unknown at the start of the game.

What matters to ordinary people is what followed:

- regions stopped exchanging life and memory;
- some landscapes began crystallizing;
- some forests became predatory;
- old roads collapsed or changed direction;
- isolated settlements learned to survive by exploiting whatever fragment of the World Vein remained beneath them;
- beings that once depended on large ecological networks were forced into smaller, unstable communities.

The world did not end.

It became **fragmented, hungry, and territorial**.

---

# 3. The Living Caravan

The player's home is a rare mobile organism known only in fragments of old records as a **Living Sanctuary**.

The inhabitants simply call it:

> **the Caravan**

It is simultaneously:

- shelter;
- organism;
- home;
- route;
- memory archive;
- defense system.

The Caravan grows rather than being constructed.

Its rooms, roots, membranes, lanterns, platforms, and storage organs respond to the people living inside it.

It does not speak in human language.

Instead it expresses itself through:
- changes in temperature;
- dreams;
- pulsation;
- altered pathways;
- sudden blooms;
- instinctive reactions between inhabitants.

The player is the **Keeper** — the person capable of interpreting the Caravan's pulse and making high-level choices for it.

The Keeper does not micromanage every inhabitant in battle.

The Keeper decides:
- who is exposed;
- who is protected;
- which relationships are reinforced;
- what the Caravan prioritizes;
- when to commit.

After commitment, the ecosystem reacts on its own.

That is the fiction behind autobattle.

---

# 4. Why the Caravan must travel

The Caravan cannot remain in one place indefinitely.

It survives by finding and absorbing **Vein Wells** — places where fragments of the old World Vein still surface.

A Vein Well might be:
- a moonlit spring;
- a buried root-city;
- a crystal wound;
- a living ruin;
- a battlefield saturated with memory;
- a shrine built around an ancient organ.

Each Well provides something different:
- nourishment;
- memory;
- new growth;
- healing;
- dangerous mutations;
- access to forgotten paths.

But drawing from a Well leaves a trace.

That trace attracts:
- states that want to weaponize Living Sanctuaries;
- hunters who profit from harvesting them;
- cults that worship or fear them;
- creatures that instinctively attack competing ecosystems.

The Caravan therefore lives by a paradox:

> **To survive, it must reveal itself.  
> To survive being revealed, it must keep moving.**

This gives travel narrative pressure without yet locking the permanent run-map structure.

---

# 5. The Heartseed

At the center of the Caravan is a living organ called the **Heartseed**.

It is not a power core.

It is closer to:
- a nervous system;
- a memory knot;
- a circulatory heart;
- a shared dream.

Every long-term inhabitant gradually becomes connected to it.

This connection explains why characters can:
- sense one another's danger;
- develop automatic protective behavior;
- accelerate each other's abilities;
- react to injuries before receiving direct orders;
- form Resonances.

The Heartseed does not erase individuality.

It makes individuality **matter more**, because one person's habits can become part of the Caravan's behavior.

---

# 6. Resonance

**Resonance** is the visible behavioral relationship between two or more members of the Caravan.

It is not simply “synergy +10%.”

A Resonance forms when:
- two people repeatedly survive together;
- one person learns another's instinct;
- fear, affection, guilt, duty, dependence, or trust becomes predictable enough for the Heartseed to reinforce it.

Examples already represented in the prototype:

### Vael + Seris — Guardian Eclipse
When Seris becomes the focus of a lethal ranged threat, Vael recognizes the pattern and intercepts before being ordered.

### Vael + Mirel — Ironbloom Oath
When Vael's condition sharply deteriorates, Mirel prioritizes him with an emergency recovery response.

### Seris + Mirel — Moonflower Bloom
When Mirel successfully stabilizes an ally, the reduced biological stress allows Seris to accelerate her ritual.

The important principle:

> **A bond changes behavior, not merely numbers.**

---

# 7. Death, loss, and restoration — world logic only

The permanent mechanics are not locked yet.

Worldbuilding establishes only the following facts:

- the Heartseed remembers people;
- severe injury can damage the connection between a person and the Caravan;
- complete separation or death can leave an **Empty Pulse** — a persistent absence in the ecosystem;
- rare methods exist to rebuild a damaged connection;
- such restoration is never clean or free.

Possible costs in the world include:
- a large quantity of stored memory;
- sacrificing access to a Vein Well;
- damaging another growth of the Caravan;
- surrendering years of accumulated adaptation;
- taking a dangerous route;
- making a pact with an external power.

These are narrative possibilities, not locked mechanics.

The emotional rule is already locked:

> **Replacing someone can be cheaper. Saving them can still be the choice the player wants to make.**

# 8. Major powers

## 8.1 The Glass Directorate
A wealthy coalition of fortress-cities that survived the Severance by refining crystallized World Vein material.

Public doctrine:
> instability must be contained.

Actual strategic interest:
- capture Living Sanctuaries;
- dissect how they sustain mobile ecosystems;
- reproduce their adaptive behavior in controllable form.

The Directorate does not view itself as evil.

Its cities protect millions of people.

From its perspective, allowing a powerful autonomous living sanctuary to wander freely is reckless.

This gives the enemy faction a credible motive rather than “evil empire.”

### Directorate battlefield language
- crystal restraints;
- targeting marks;
- suppression tools;
- containment squads;
- behavioral prediction;
- specialized hunters.

Current prototype enemies can belong to a Directorate pursuit unit.

---

## 8.2 The Root Choir
A distributed intelligence living across several ancient forests.

It remembers pieces of the pre-Severance World Vein.

It does not think like a person.

The Root Choir may:
- shelter the Caravan;
- demand something in exchange;
- refuse passage because one inhabitant is “wrong”;
- recognize memories the characters themselves have forgotten.

The Choir is neither ally nor enemy.

It is a living region with preferences.

---

## 8.3 The Ashbound Freeholds
Independent towns and migrating communities built in regions where the World Vein no longer functions reliably.

They trade:
- food;
- weapons;
- rumors;
- medicine;
- route knowledge.

Some Freeholds see the Caravan as:
- miracle;
- opportunity;
- threat;
- refuge.

This is where quieter character-focused stories can happen.

---

## 8.4 The Pale Pilgrims
A religious movement that believes the Severance was not a disaster but an unfinished transformation.

They believe attachment is the source of suffering.

They intentionally cut bonds, identities, and memories to become “light enough to cross the dead world.”

Their philosophy directly opposes the Caravan's core principle.

The Caravan says:
> connection makes survival meaningful.

The Pilgrims say:
> connection is what makes loss unbearable.

They are therefore ideal thematic antagonists for later attachment/loss arcs.

---

# 9. The first region — Moonscar Wood

The tutorial takes place in **Moonscar Wood**.

Moonscar Wood is a forest where moonlight remains trapped in old World Vein crystals beneath the soil.

Visual identity:
- deep blue foliage;
- silver leaves;
- warm survivor lanterns;
- cyan mineral growth;
- violet fungal blooms;
- roots growing around old stone paths;
- areas that appear peaceful until disturbed.

Why the Caravan is here:

The Heartseed woke recently and is unstable.

A Vein Well called the **Moonwell** may be able to stabilize its pulse.

Why the enemy is here:

The Glass Directorate detected the same pulse.

A small containment team arrives first.

This creates the opening situation:

> **Night 1 — reach the Moonwell before the Directorate identifies what the Caravan is.**

# 10. Current prototype enemies in world context

## Crystal Breaker
Directorate breaching organism / weapon-beast.

Role:
- smash barriers;
- force a defensive front to reveal itself;
- break living walls before capture teams enter.

Battle behavior:
- obvious frontal violence;
- high commitment;
- readable wind-up.

---

## Blind Hunter
Directorate specialist trained to attack by resonance signature rather than sight.

Role:
- ignore the obvious frontline;
- identify fragile high-value targets;
- disrupt rituals and support behavior.

The name “Blind Hunter” refers to the fact that ordinary sight is deliberately suppressed to prevent visual decoys from confusing the unit.

---

## Stigmatized Hound
A pursuit beast marked with crystal scars that react to biological stress.

Role:
- detect wounded targets;
- chase weakness;
- prevent escape.

This explains the prototype behavior “track the weakest ally.”

# 11. The current three inhabitants

These biographies are **working canon** and can be refined later.

## Vael — Guardian
Core impression:
> Someone who makes danger feel smaller by stepping into it.

World role:
Vael has a highly developed threat-sense connection to the Heartseed.

He does not protect because “tank is his class.”

He protects because the Caravan has learned through him that:
> when something dangerous approaches, Vael moves first.

Character tension:
He is becoming too willing to define his value through what he can endure.

Future emotional risk:
The player may have to choose between using his reliability and refusing to let him destroy himself for the ecosystem.

---

## Seris — Moon Ritualist
Core impression:
> Delicate-looking, composed, and terrifying once allowed to finish what she started.

World role:
Seris can shape stored moonlight in Moonscar crystal into large ritual effects.

Her power requires uninterrupted concentration.

This makes her naturally dependent on other inhabitants.

Character tension:
She dislikes needing protection, even though her strongest abilities literally require trust.

Future emotional risk:
A strong Resonance can make her more powerful while making the possibility of losing her protector much more frightening.

---

## Mirel — Bloom Healer
Core impression:
> Gentle behavior built around a frightening ability to decide what should keep growing.

World role:
Mirel reads biological stress as patterns of growth and decay.

She can push damaged tissue toward recovery by forcing dormant regenerative pathways to bloom.

Character tension:
Healing is not emotionally neutral to her.

She can feel what the body is giving up in order to recover.

Future emotional risk:
She may eventually know that saving someone now will cause a cost later — and still want to save them.

# 12. The first narrative arc

## Arc title
**The First Pulse**

### Beat 1 — The Caravan wakes
The player experiences the first night after the Heartseed becomes fully active.

The characters already know one another, but their relationship with the awakened Caravan is new.

### Beat 2 — Someone heard it
A pulse from the Heartseed was detected outside the Caravan.

The party realizes they are being tracked.

### Beat 3 — First pursuit
A Directorate containment unit blocks the Moonwell route.

This is the current tutorial battle.

### Beat 4 — The ecosystem responds
The player learns:
- intent;
- formation;
- commitment;
- automatic protection;
- healing;
- ritual payoff.

### Beat 5 — Resonance becomes visible
After surviving together, one pair's automatic behavior becomes strong enough to be recognized as Resonance.

This is the current second tutorial.

### Beat 6 — The Moonwell is not empty
At the destination, something inside the Root Choir recognizes the Heartseed.

It calls the Caravan by a name nobody in the party knows.

This is the first larger mystery hook.

# 13. Opening mystery stack

The first hours should answer small questions while opening larger ones.

Immediate:
- who is attacking us?
- how do we survive?
- how does this Caravan react?

Short-term:
- why did the Heartseed awaken now?
- why can the Keeper hear it?
- why is the Directorate interested?

Long-term:
- what caused the Severance?
- are Living Sanctuaries remnants of the old world or something created after it?
- why does the Root Choir recognize this Caravan?
- has the Caravan existed before under another identity?

Do not front-load all answers.

---

# 14. Tone rules

The narrative should avoid:
- lore-dump dialogue;
- generic chosen-one prophecy;
- villains who attack “because evil”;
- characters explaining mechanics in unnatural language;
- constant tragedy with no warmth.

The Caravan should also contain:
- teasing;
- domestic routines;
- food;
- arguments;
- sleeping habits;
- small acts of care;
- jealousy;
- jokes;
- fear of losing one another.

The darker the world becomes, the more important the Caravan's warmth becomes.

---

# 16. Narrative implementation rule

Worldbuilding is successful only if it changes the player's experience.

Every region / faction / character concept should eventually create one or more of:

- a battle behavior;
- a route decision;
- a relationship decision;
- a visual motif;
- a recovery / loss consequence;
- a memorable camp scene;
- a choice the player hates having to make.

Do not create encyclopedia lore with no gameplay consequence.
