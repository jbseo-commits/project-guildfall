# CHARACTER BIBLE — INITIAL CAST

> **Status: PROVISIONAL CHARACTERIZATION**
>
> Visual identity is already approved in broad direction.
> This document defines personality, relationship, and narrative function without changing existing gameplay roles.

---

# 1. Character rule

Every core character must be desirable for more than combat efficiency.

A player should be able to say:

> “I would keep this person even if a stronger replacement existed.”

Each character therefore needs:
- a recognizable emotional need;
- a flaw that causes friction;
- a relationship that changes over time;
- a non-combat routine;
- a visible reaction to loss;
- a reason the caravan changes if they disappear.

---

# 2. Vael

## Functional identity
**The Shield / Boundary**

Vael reacts first when danger crosses into the caravan's space.

Current combat expression:
- guard;
- intercept;
- absorb impact;
- protect concentration;
- finish / counter.

## Personality
Calm, restrained, physically decisive.

Vael is not stoic because he feels little.

He is controlled because he feels responsibility too strongly.

He has difficulty allowing others to take risks when he could take them instead.

## Emotional need
To learn that protecting someone does not mean deciding for them.

## Flaw
Vael turns affection into self-sacrifice.

He can become controlling through protection.

## Camp routine
Before resting, Vael checks:
- doors;
- straps;
- lanterns;
- sleeping areas;
- exits.

He remembers who prefers which side of the fire.

## Attachment hook
The player should notice that Vael quietly maintains everyone else's safety.

If he disappears, the caravan should feel physically less secure.

## Speech
Short sentences.

Rarely dramatic.

Examples:
> “Behind me.”
>
> “No. You were the target.”
>
> “I know you can survive it. That isn't the point.”

## Relationship — Seris
Vael protects Seris because her concentration makes her vulnerable.

Seris resents being treated as fragile.

Their arc should move from:
- protection as unilateral action;
to:
- protection as trusted coordination.

## Relationship — Mirel
Mirel notices the injuries Vael hides.

Vael dislikes becoming a patient.

Mirel is one of the few people who can make him sit still.

---

# 3. Seris

## Functional identity
**The Eye / Pattern Reader**

Seris reads:
- threat;
- timing;
- resonance;
- future possibility.

Current combat expression:
- identify danger;
- maintain concentration;
- ritual buildup;
- moon burst.

## Personality
Elegant, observant, slightly mischievous.

Seris understands more than she immediately explains.

She enjoys being right, but dislikes being needed only for answers.

## Emotional need
To be treated as a person, not as an oracle or instrument.

## Flaw
Seris can hide fear behind certainty.

When she does not know something, she may become evasive rather than admit uncertainty.

## Camp routine
Seris records changing patterns:
- moonlight;
- footsteps;
- pulse rhythms;
- repeated conversations;
- routes the caravan should not take again.

She occasionally draws things that have not happened yet.

## Attachment hook
The player comes to trust Seris's interpretation of danger.

If she disappears, the world should feel less understandable.

## Speech
Precise, dry, occasionally teasing.

Examples:
> “That was the bad possibility.”
>
> “There are three routes. I dislike all of them.”
>
> “Vael, I saw the arrow.”
>
> “Yes. I also saw you ignore me.”

## Relationship — Vael
Seris appreciates Vael's reliability but hates being protected without consent.

Her best combat moments should eventually look coordinated rather than accidental.

## Relationship — Mirel
Seris trusts Mirel emotionally more than she admits.

Mirel can tell when Seris is pretending certainty.

---

# 4. Mirel

## Functional identity
**The Blood / Repair**

Mirel reads the ecosystem through damage.

Current combat expression:
- detect danger;
- identify the weakest ally;
- heal;
- stabilize;
- recover.

## Personality
Warm, attentive, stubborn.

Mirel is not naive.

She understands how fragile life is more clearly than most people.

That is why she insists on caring for it.

## Emotional need
To believe that care still matters even when it cannot save everyone.

## Flaw
Mirel can turn every failure into personal guilt.

She may spend too much to save someone because she cannot accept limits.

This makes her central to the game's attachment thesis.

## Camp routine
Mirel tends:
- flowers;
- wounds;
- food;
- damaged caravan tissue;
- small personal objects others forget to repair.

She dislikes throwing broken things away.

## Attachment hook
Mirel is the character most likely to make the player feel that recovery is possible.

If she disappears, the caravan should feel colder and more disposable.

## Speech
Gentle but direct.

Examples:
> “Sit.”
>
> “You can argue after I stop the bleeding.”
>
> “It can grow again.”
>
> “I didn't say it would be cheap.”

## Relationship — Vael
Mirel is frustrated by Vael's habit of treating his body as expendable armor.

She knows exactly how often he lies about being fine.

## Relationship — Seris
Mirel can tell when Seris is afraid.

She does not expose Seris publicly.

She simply stays nearby.

---

# 5. Initial triangle

The first trio should form a complete emotional system.

## Vael
Protects the outside boundary.

## Seris
Interprets what is coming.

## Mirel
Repairs what gets through.

This mirrors the Living Caravan itself:
- boundary;
- perception;
- restoration.

That makes their absence mechanically and emotionally legible.

---

# 6. Relationship progression

Relationships should not only grant bonuses.

They should alter visible behavior.

Examples:

## Vael ↔ Seris
Early:
- Vael intercepts automatically.
- Seris complains.

Later:
- Seris shifts before the shot.
- Vael moves into the prepared angle.
- the same protection becomes coordinated.

## Vael ↔ Mirel
Early:
- Mirel heals Vael after he overextends.

Later:
- Vael changes how much damage he accepts because he trusts Mirel's limits.

## Seris ↔ Mirel
Early:
- Mirel stabilizes Seris after ritual strain.

Later:
- Mirel deliberately times healing to accelerate Seris's ritual.

The player should be able to see relationship growth during battle.

---

# 7. Character loss presentation

If one of the trio is absent:

## Vael absent
- camp perimeter feels open;
- doors remain unchecked;
- Seris's concentration becomes riskier;
- Mirel may move toward danger more often.

## Seris absent
- route forecasts become uncertain;
- enemy intent information may degrade;
- Vael has fewer warnings;
- Mirel becomes more anxious before travel.

## Mirel absent
- persistent wounds matter more;
- repair visuals disappear;
- Vael's self-sacrifice becomes more dangerous;
- Seris may become quieter.

Loss should affect atmosphere before statistics.

---

# 8. Character growth rule

Do not express growth only through:
- level;
- rarity;
- stars;
- larger numbers.

Growth should also appear as:
- new battle reactions;
- changed idle behavior;
- new camp routines;
- relationship barks;
- new willingness to trust;
- scars;
- changed poses;
- shared rituals.

A veteran character should look like someone who has lived with the caravan.

---

# 9. Recruitment rule

Future characters should not enter as anonymous loot.

A recruit should require at least one memorable moment:
- rescue;
- negotiation;
- conflict;
- debt;
- mutual need;
- deliberate invitation.

Joining the caravan should mean:
> “We made room for someone.”

not:
> “We pulled a new unit.”

The permanent recruitment system remains open.

---

# 10. Immediate narrative implementation opportunity

Without changing core combat systems, future content can strengthen attachment by adding:

- short pre-battle camp lines;
- post-battle injury reactions;
- relationship-specific intercept / heal barks;
- one persistent personal object per character;
- visible empty-space treatment when someone is unavailable;
- a first injury that persists across at least two scenes.

These are lower-risk narrative additions that can coexist with the current combat-art loop.
