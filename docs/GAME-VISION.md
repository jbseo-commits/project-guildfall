# GAME VISION

## Product thesis

A fantasy strategy roguelite where the player experiences two complementary pleasures:

1. **Authorship** — “I built this.”
2. **Payoff** — “Now I get to watch it work.”

The game should not turn into a passive simulator. The automatic battle exists to make prior decisions legible and emotionally rewarding.

## Core emotional loop

**READ** — understand the coming threat or opportunity.

**BUILD** — compose a response through units, positions, roles, commands, items, traits, or another original strategic language.

**COMMIT** — lock the plan. This moment should carry tension.

**WATCH** — the party executes. Little or no micro; high spectacle and high readability.

**DEBRIEF** — communicate why the outcome happened.

**ADAPT** — make a meaningful change for the next fight.

## Design guardrails

### The build must be visible in battle
If the player drafts “chain lightning + wet + conductor,” the battle should visibly show a chain reaction. Hidden spreadsheet bonuses are not enough.

### Automatic does not mean opaque
The player must understand targeting, timing, role behavior, and why a key event happened.

### Decisions should create behavior, not only numbers
A new trait should ideally change movement, target priority, sequencing, formation, resource flow, or combo behavior.

### Short feedback loops first
Before a 40-minute run exists, a 60–120 second repeatable micro-loop should already be fun.

### Fewer, more legible units
Early prototypes should prefer 3–5 meaningful actors over 12 tiny units with unreadable interactions.

## Open identity space

We have deliberately not chosen whether the player leads:

- a guild/company,
- a doomed expedition,
- a magical academy squad,
- a monster caravan,
- a dungeon ecosystem,
- a mercenary theater troupe,
- a pact of summoned spirits,
- or something stranger.

Decision 001 chooses this.
