# NARRATIVE VERTICAL SLICE — DAY 1: THE LEAVING NIGHT

> **Status:** PLAYABLE NARRATIVE BLUEPRINT V1
>
> This document converts the Living Caravan world bible into the first tutorial/story sequence.
> It is designed around the current playable mechanics rather than requiring a new combat engine.

---

# 1. Purpose

The first 15–25 minutes must prove four things at once:

1. the caravan is a home;
2. Vael, Seris, and Mirel are people worth caring about;
3. the player's preparation changes automatic battle behavior;
4. the outside world is dangerous because it can take specific people away.

The tutorial should not feel like a system lesson with story pasted on top.

The story itself should create the need for each mechanic.

---

# 2. Opening image

Night.

Lanternwood is too quiet.

Inside the caravan:
- warm fire;
- hanging cloth;
- tools;
- food;
- half-finished repairs;
- personal objects;
- three characters in the same space.

The player should understand **home** before understanding combat.

No lore crawl.

No prophecy.

No giant title card explaining the Stillfall.

The first emotional fact is:

> **These people live here together.**

---

# 3. Opening character beats

## Vael

Vael is awake before everyone else.

Not because of a sound.

Because the outer wall of the sanctuary tightened.

Opening line candidate:

> "Don't pack yet. If I'm wrong, you can complain after sunrise."

This establishes:
- vigilance;
- dry confidence;
- protection.

---

## Seris

Seris has been mapping a route by moonlight.

The map contains a road no one else can see.

Opening line candidate:

> "It was there again. The road behind the hill. The one that isn't there."

This establishes:
- Moon Memory;
- uncertainty;
- a reason the enemy wants Seris.

---

## Mirel

Mirel is trying to keep a plant alive that keeps blooming toward an empty chair.

Opening line candidate:

> "It only does this when we're about to lose something."

Then Mirel smiles and dismisses it as a joke.

This quietly introduces:
- relational ecology;
- loss anxiety;
- uncanny warmth.

---

# 4. Inciting incident

The Hearthheart reacts.

Not with a voice.

The caravan does three things:
- lanterns dim on the eastern side;
- a corridor contracts;
- the floor gives one deep pulse.

Vael immediately understands:

> someone is approaching with hostile intent.

The player is shown the first enemy group.

The White Index has not come to kill everyone.

They have come for **Seris**.

That specificity matters.

The first tutorial battle becomes:

> **Protect one of ours from being taken.**

---

# 5. Tutorial step 1 — READ

Current mechanic:
- enemy intent reading.

Narrative framing:
Vael asks the Keeper to look at what the attackers are actually trying to do.

The enemy group:

### Crystal Breaker
Intent:
- smash the front;
- create an opening.

### Blind Hunter
Intent:
- ignore the strongest defender;
- mark Seris in the rear.

### Scar Hound
Intent:
- exploit whoever is exposed after the formation breaks.

Tutorial text should be character-driven.

Instead of:
> "Red arrow means front attack."

Use:
> **Vael:** "The big one wants me looking at it. The hooded one doesn't."

Then the UI highlights:
- Breaker → front;
- Hunter → rear.

Player learns threat reading through concern for Seris.

---

# 6. Tutorial step 2 — PLAN

Current mechanic:
- FRONT / CORE / VEIL placement.

Narrative framing:
The caravan is too narrow to fight as a conventional squad.

Characters choose positions in the living passage.

The player decides who stands where.

Important:
The tutorial should show a clearly tempting wrong arrangement.

Example:
- putting Seris in front may increase immediate cast access or readability;
- but exposes Seris to the Breaker.

The player should feel:
> "I understand why this is dangerous."

Not:
> "The tutorial says this slot is bad."

---

# 7. Tutorial step 3 — COMMIT

This is a ritualized emotional moment.

The caravan seals the passage.

The player cannot micro after this.

Suggested UI phrase:

> **"문을 닫는다."**

Secondary line:

> 이제 서로를 믿어야 합니다.

This is stronger fictionally than a generic "Start Battle" button.

The exact final wording can be iterated in UI.

---

# 8. Tutorial step 4 — WATCH

The existing Combat Motion V2 chain fits the narrative:

1. Breaker telegraphs Vael.
2. Breaker lunges.
3. Vael absorbs the opening impact.
4. Hunter marks Seris.
5. Vael recognizes the threat.
6. Vael INTERCEPTS.
7. Mirel sees Vael become the weakest member.
8. Mirel heals Vael.
9. Seris keeps concentration because Vael bought time.
10. Seris completes Moon Ritual.
11. Moon Burst collapses the enemy formation.

The battle is the story.

No cutscene should interrupt this chain.

---

# 9. Tutorial step 5 — UNDERSTAND

After the fight, do not only show damage numbers.

Show the relational chain.

Example debrief:

> **세리스가 살아남은 이유**
>
> 추적자가 세리스를 노림  
> → 베일이 대신 맞음  
> → 베일의 체력이 가장 낮아짐  
> → 미렐이 베일을 치유  
> → 세리스 집중 유지  
> → 월광 의식 완성

The player should be able to point to their earlier formation decision and say:

> **"내가 저 관계를 만들었다."**

---

# 10. First emotional aftermath

After the victory, the mood should not immediately become triumphant.

Vael is injured.

Mirel is angry.

Not at the enemy.

At Vael.

Suggested exchange:

**Mirel**
> "다음에도 그렇게 끼어들 거야?"

**Vael**
> "응."

**Mirel**
> "그럴 줄 알았어."

**Seris**
> "…미안."

**Vael**
> "그 말은 다음에 안 맞았을 때 해."

The point:
- Vael's protective behavior is not just a skill;
- Mirel's healing creates emotional labor;
- Seris feels the cost of being protected.

This seeds attachment.

---

# 11. Discovery after battle

The defeated White Index unit carries a retrieval order.

Not a generic bounty.

It contains:
- Seris's name or identifying mark;
- an old sanctuary symbol;
- a route designation that Seris recognizes but has never seen consciously.

The order implies:

> The White Index knew the caravan would wake tonight.

This creates the first mystery.

---

# 12. First post-battle choice

The player gets a story choice with mechanical meaning.

Three route responses:

## A. Follow the erased road

Seris believes the invisible road leads somewhere safe.

Theme:
- trust Seris;
- pursue mystery.

Risk:
- unknown terrain;
- high Echo exposure.

## B. Seek a Hearthless camp

Mirel wants supplies and information.

Theme:
- community;
- recovery.

Risk:
- slower;
- may bring pursuers closer.

## C. Cross the old quarry

Vael prefers a route with defensible terrain.

Theme:
- safety;
- control.

Risk:
- resource-poor;
- more direct combat.

This is not yet the permanent run map.

It is a narrative prototype for D004 evidence.

---

# 13. Tutorial 2 — RESONANCE

The second encounter introduces relationship-based automatic behavior.

The player selects two characters to create a Resonance.

Narratively, this is not "equip synergy."

It is:
> the Keeper chooses which relationship to deliberately reinforce before the next danger.

Examples already prototyped:

### Vael + Seris — Guardian Eclipse
Seris becomes a protected ritual anchor.

### Vael + Mirel — Ironbloom Oath
Mirel responds aggressively to Vael's self-sacrificial behavior.

### Seris + Mirel — Moonflower Bloom
Mirel's restoration accelerates Seris's ritual state.

The tutorial should frame this as:
> "who are you asking to rely on whom?"

---

# 14. The first failure lesson

The first chapter should eventually include one encounter that is intentionally difficult to solve perfectly.

The goal is not to kill a character.

The goal is to teach:
> protecting everyone has a cost.

Possible result:
- one character receives a persistent injury;
- the caravan loses a room;
- a route closes;
- the Hearthheart consumes stored Sap to repair someone.

This is the first seed of the later attachment / inefficiency thesis.

Permanent loss mechanics remain open.

---

# 15. First camp after danger

After the second encounter, the caravan interior changes.

Examples:
- Vael's resting space is closer to Mirel;
- Seris's map wall has expanded;
- the Hearthheart has grown a protective rib around the doorway;
- the empty-chair flower has stopped blooming.

The game should physically show that combat changed home.

This is critical.

A run must not feel like:
> battle screen → menu → battle screen.

The sanctuary should remember.

---

# 16. The chapter-ending hook

At the end of the first playable chapter, Seris opens the recovered White Index order again.

The route symbol on the paper matches a sealed door inside the Hearthheart.

The door has always been there.

No one noticed the symbol before.

The Hearthheart reacts to the paper.

One pulse.

Then another.

From behind the sealed door:

> a fourth pulse answers.

Cut.

This establishes:
- the caravan contains something unknown;
- the White Index may know more about it;
- the player's home itself is part of the mystery.

---

# 17. What the player should know after the vertical slice

Without reading a codex, the player should understand:

- the caravan is alive;
- it must move;
- Vael protects;
- Mirel repairs;
- Seris remembers impossible routes;
- relationships create automatic combat behavior;
- the White Index wants something from the caravan;
- the caravan has a hidden past;
- losing a character would hurt more than losing combat power.

The player should NOT yet need to know:
- the exact origin of the Stillfall;
- the full history of the White Index;
- what the Hearthheart truly is;
- the final goal of the entire game.

Mystery is useful.

Confusion is not.

---

# 19. Narrative QA gate

The first chapter is GREEN only if a new player can answer:

- Who is being threatened?
- Why does Vael intervene?
- Why does Mirel heal Vael?
- Why can Seris finish the ritual?
- Why does the caravan need to leave?
- What does the White Index want?
- Why would losing one of these characters matter?

If the player only remembers UI instructions, the narrative integration failed.

---

# 20. North-star line

The first chapter should leave the player with this feeling:

> **"They came for one person. The whole caravan moved to keep them."**
